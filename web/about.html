How it works

Claude does the hard language work before class, where there is time and network. A person approves everything children will hear. In class, small models on the device only recognise answers and pick from approved lines.

Where AI runs

DraftClaude Opus 5.5 turns the week's lesson into a pack: a story, 3-6 items with colour-button choices, hints, the words children may say, likely questions with answers, and near-miss questions the pack must not answer. Output is schema-validated JSON.online, before class
ScoreA second, independent Claude call scores the draft on five criteria (lesson fit, age, home-language naturalness, one clear answer, safety) and lists issues and phrases a native speaker should check.online
CheckCode checks every pack: item counts, one correct colour per item, script, empty lines, prompt length. Errors block approval.no AI
ApproveA teacher or local speaker edits any line and approves. Low scores need a written note. Only approved packs are voiced and can run.person
Answer recognitionVosk speech recognition on the device, restricted to the words that item expects. Unclear speech or low confidence triggers a re-prompt; a child is never marked wrong on a recognition error. Colour buttons always work.offline
QuestionsA multilingual sentence-embedding model on the device matches a child's question to the pack's FAQ. Gate 1: it must be close to a known phrasing. Gate 2: it must be closer to that than to the near-miss questions. Otherwise: "ask your teacher". The model selects; it never writes speech.offline
Next-day noteWhen the teacher's phone has network, Claude turns the group-level log into a short note. Code then checks every count, item and question in the note against the log.optional sync

Guardrails

  • Children only hear lines a person approved, voiced before class.
  • No open-ended generation in class; questions outside the pack go to the teacher.
  • Recognition limited to expected answers; uncertainty means re-prompt, not a wrong mark.
  • No child audio is stored or sent. The log has counts, timings and question text, no names.
  • Claude's outputs are schema-validated, independently scored, and checked by code before use.

What we tested and changed

  • Our proposal used a small on-device LLM to judge children's questions. On Hindi and Marathi test questions, qwen2.5 1.5B, qwen2.5 3B and gemma3 1B each got 4 of 8 right. A multilingual embedding model with Claude-written near-miss questions got all 8 (and matched in Python and in the browser), so the device uses that.
  • The Hindi speech model knows most Marathi number words (दोन, पाच, सहा, दहा) but not Marathi colour words (निळा, हिरवा, पिवळा), so colour buttons stay as the reliable fallback.
